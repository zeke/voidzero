## A Looped-Vocal Art-Pop Style

A singer and multi-instrumentalist whose work is built from real-time
looping: layered, chanted vocals stacked into call-and-response choirs over
lo-fi percussion, ukulele, and bass. It sits loosely under art pop and
worldbeat, with strong polyrhythmic, Afrobeat-influenced rhythm. The voice
is both the lead instrument and the main textural material.

## What the music sounds like

Recurring traits run through the work:

- Vocal layering as architecture. Many takes of a single elastic voice,
  from holler to coo, looped and stacked into rhythmic, choir-like beds
  rather than simple lead-and-backing.
- Percussion-forward and polyrhythmic. Busy, interlocking hand percussion
  and lo-fi drums drive the arrangements; rhythm carries as much meaning as
  melody.
- Handmade and lo-fi. Loop pedals, ukulele, and bass with rough-edged
  recording give a raw, built-in-a-room feel.
- Deliberately off-kilter. Energetic and playful one moment, tense and
  dissonant the next.

## The film dimension

Applied to film scoring, the same toolkit (looped chant and clattering
percussion) becomes nervous, disorienting texture. Rather than cushioning
scenes with conventional orchestral warmth, the score amplifies unease and
dislocation: jittery and off-kilter, tense and playful at once. It suits
surreal, satirical material that lurches between comedy and dread, an
erratic and ecstatic medley of instruments and vocal layering.

## A spectrum across the work

The same looping instinct stretches across a range:

- Song-forward pop: tight, hook-driven tracks with full verses and
  choruses, the vocal loops serving a catchy tune.
- Chant-driven rhythmic workouts: percussion and stacked voices building by
  accumulation into something closer to communal ritual.
- Abstract scoring mode: the loops and percussion collaged into anxious,
  textural underscore that mirrors a jangled nervous system.

## One-line summary

Rhythmic, vocal-looped art pop with Afrobeat-influenced percussion, where a
single layered voice becomes both melody and texture, turning, in film work,
into deliberately disorienting, anxious underscore.
