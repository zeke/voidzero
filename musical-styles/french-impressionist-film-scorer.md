## A Film-Scoring Style

A classically trained composer, conductor, and multi-instrumentalist
steeped in early-twentieth-century French impressionism, jazz, and world
music, who scores everything from small art-house dramas to large studio
blockbusters. The training shows: scores favor color, texture, and
restraint over bombast, supporting the image rather than competing with it.

## What the scores sound like

Recurring traits run through the work:

- Clockwork ostinatos. Small repeating melodic-rhythmic cells, often in
  piano, harp, plucked or struck strings, and woodwinds, that tick along
  like a wound mechanism, generating momentum and quiet anticipation rather
  than big emotional swells.
- Music-box precision. Metronomically exact rhythms and child-like chiming
  ideas (celeste, glockenspiel, pizzicato), tightly controlled and a little
  toy-like.
- Woodwind- and chamber-forward color. A flautist's ear gives reeds and
  flutes unusual prominence, with transparent orchestration even inside a
  full orchestra and frequent chamber-scale forces.
- Melancholy elegance. A wistful, bittersweet, reserved lyricism inherited
  from an older European concert tradition.
- Miniaturism. Cues are often very short and built from modular repeating
  cells rather than long sustained lines.

## The chameleon

The same core voice is recast per project. Tense thrillers get propulsive,
modal ostinatos that build dread through repetition; prestige dramas get
intimate piano-and-strings dignity; even large blockbusters lean toward
darkness, mystery, and atmosphere over triumphal heroics.

## A spectrum across the work

The handmade-precision sensibility stretches across a range:

- At one end, playful, geometric, music-box scoring for highly stylized,
  symmetrical visual worlds: plucked strings, whistling, marches, and a
  toy-box precision that mirrors the framing.
- In the middle, region-specific folk palettes used like a passport stamp
  for a setting: a Central and Eastern European folk lute ensemble, zithers,
  hammered dulcimers, and alpine mountain horns interleaved with borrowed
  concert pieces; elsewhere, a Japanese drum-and-flute percussion tradition
  driving relentless martial rhythms.
- At the other end, restrained dramatic scoring where transparent chamber
  textures and a single recurring motif carry the emotional weight without
  ever tipping into sentimentality.

## One-line summary

Colorist, miniaturist film scoring at blockbuster scale: clockwork rhythms,
woodwind-forward chamber orchestration, and refined impressionist harmony,
elegant, melancholy, and restrained, with each project wearing a distinct
instrumental palette over the same understated, slightly anxious lyricism.
