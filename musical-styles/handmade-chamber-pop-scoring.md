## A Film-Scoring Style

A multi-instrumentalist, songwriter, and producer who came up through
power-pop and singer-songwriter circles before moving into film scoring.
That pop-craft background shapes the approach: even orchestral cues are
built like songs, with strong melodic hooks rather than ambient texture.

## What the scores sound like

Recurring traits run through the work:

- Vintage, handmade, slightly broken. A signature reliance on early analog
  sampling keyboards such as the Chamberlin and the Optigan, which produce
  near-but-not-quite-real emulations of strings, flutes, and choirs. The
  result is warm, woozy, and faintly out-of-tune, like a half-remembered
  dream or a music box running down.
- One-person orchestration. Most instruments are played by the composer
  (piano, guitar, bass, drums, cello, trombone, harmonium, vibraphone,
  celeste), giving the music a personal, "constructed in a room" intimacy
  rather than a big studio-orchestra gloss.
- Toy-box and percussion textures. Tack piano, glockenspiel, marimba, hand
  percussion, and tabla.
- Melody first, emotion forward. Tonal, lyrical, often melancholy but sweet,
  unafraid of sentiment.
- Song/score blurring. Because the composer is a songwriter, the scores
  frequently include full vocal songs alongside instrumental cues, and the
  licensed material is curated so an album plays as one piece.

## The live dimension

Years of improvised, no-setlist solo club shows, looping a song into being
from drums up through piano, bass, guitar, and vocals, explain the
spontaneous, layered, "assembled in real time" feel of the scores. The same
looping and jigsaw instinct shows up as music that grows by accumulation,
including theme-and-variation reprises where one melody returns recast for
different instruments.

## A spectrum across the work

The same handmade chamber-pop sensibility stretches across a range:

- At one end, an anxious, percussive, abstract mode: clattering hand drums,
  tack piano, and harmonium collaged to mirror a jangled nervous system,
  resolving into lush romance.
- In the middle, dreamlike instrumental memory-music, where the worn,
  out-of-tune keyboard textures make recollection feel tactile.
- At the other end, fully song-forward work, where the cues resolve into
  hummable acoustic-guitar pop with real verses and choruses, room for comic
  genre pastiche, and a single motif recast as both a sung tune and a string
  quartet.

## One-line summary

Melodic, handmade chamber-pop scoring built on vintage analog keyboards,
warm and a little melancholy, with a deliberately worn, dreamlike texture
that makes emotion and memory feel tactile.
